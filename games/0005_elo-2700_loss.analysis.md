### Shogun Sensei — chess analysis

**Game story:** Chezz gained a +246 cp advantage early but collapsed catastrophically in the middlegame. The critical failure occurred at ply 31 (move 16. b4), where we weakened our queenside-castled king with a pawn advance; our engine misjudged this position 345 cp—evaluating +174 when it was -171. We remained systematically overoptimistic while Stockfish exploited resulting tactical weaknesses, leading to checkmate.

**Key moments:**

1. **Ply 31 (16. b4 vs. Rd3):** King safety blindness. Played b4 in front of queenside-castled king (Kc1). Engine +174 vs. objective -171 (345 cp error). Theme: **pawn shield compromised**. **Evaluation blindness**.
2. **Plies 35–65:** Engine consistently overestimated unsafe positions: Qb3 (+1 vs. -102), Rhe1 (-25 vs. -335), Nc3 (-43 vs. -200). After ply 31 destabilized king, tactical threats cascaded. **Evaluation blindness**—failed to properly weight king danger + incoming tactics.
3. **Ply 77+:** Eval crashes (-557, -625, -687). Material/mating threats unmask—lost by ply 132.

**Suggestions (ranked):**

1. **Penalize b-pawn advances in front of queenside-castled king** (b4/b5 from b2/b3: -75 mg penalty). Engine failed to see b4 wrecks pawn shield, inviting tactics on c-file and long diagonal. Evidence: ply 31. Expected impact: high.
2. **Increase king-safety weight in middlegame** — double safety term weight when king is castled in semi-open file with <3 escape squares. Our systematic +100–300 cp overestimation (plies 31–65) shows safety terms underweight in pressure positions. Evidence: ply 31–65. Expected impact: high.
3. **Deepen search in unsafe king positions** — allocate extra depth when king exposed to multi-piece attack. The -25 vs. -335 error (ply 39) indicates shallow horizon for king-safety tactics. Evidence: ply 39 (Rhe1). Expected impact: medium.
