### Shogun Sensei — chess analysis

**Game story:** Clean 1-0 victory vs Elo 1500 Stockfish with strong Bc4+Nf3+Ne4 middlegame setup against the Scandinavian. We won via checkmate after exploiting Black's weakened kingside pawn structure (doubled f-pawns from gxf6). Only one inaccuracy marred an otherwise excellent game.

**Key moments:**

- **Ply 21 (move 11): Ng3 vs Re1** — Loss: 72 cp. Theme: *Rook centralization vs piece retreat*. We moved Ne4 away from queen attack to g3 instead of activating our rook on the e-file. After Ng3, Black played Qf4 with more tactical resources, dropping our true eval from +126 to +54. **Evaluation blind spot**: we thought +110 but true position was +126 before the move, then significantly underestimated how much allowing Black's queen activity (Qf4 next move) would deteriorate our advantage. At depth 17 we should have seen this cascade.
- **Ply 25 onwards (Re1 finally played):** Once we eventually played Re1 (ply 25, after 4 wasted plies), we dominated decisively. This confirms rook centralization was the critical resource.

**Suggestions (ranked):**

1. **Penalize rook passivity and bonus rook centralization in middlegame evaluation.** Reduce eval ~25–30 mg per rook on a/h files or 8th rank in open middlegame; bonus ~40 mg per rook on central files (d/e) or 7th rank. Evidence: ply 21 Re1 would have prevented Black's Qf4 tactic, but we evaluated Ng3 similarly. Expected impact: **HIGH** — rook activity is fundamental and we're missing this pattern.
2. **When opponent has active queen and you must move a piece, prioritize rook centralization over knight/bishop retreats.** The Qf5 position should trigger rook-first logic. Evidence: Ng3 + Qf4 sequence wasted tempo. Expected impact: **MED** — reinforces heuristic priority but less concrete than evaluation tuning.

### Engine Smith — engine analysis

**Diagnosis:**

1. **Ply 21 (11.Ng3) → move ordering / search depth issue → 72 cp loss**
   - Position: After 10...Qf5, White played Ng3 (eval +110, depth 17), but Re1 was better (+126 → +54).
   - Root cause: Both are quiet moves; `score_move()` scores them identically by history heuristic alone. Re1 (rook to central e-file) should rank above Ng3 (knight to flank), but without PST-delta quiet-move ordering, the engine can't distinguish them. Re1 was either searched with reduced depth (LMR) or ordered low, causing the error.
   - Clean win overall (ACPL 8), but this tactic reveals the move-ordering weakness.

**Code suggestions (ranked by expected Elo / risk):**

1. **`search.rs::score_move` — add PST-delta bonus for quiet moves** (Risk: low). For each quiet move, compute a bonus using destination minus source square tables from `eval.rs` (e.g., `MG_PST[role][dest] - MG_PST[role][src]` tapered). This recognizes that Re1 improves rook placement while Ng3 moves the knight to the flank. Expected +30–50 Elo at your horizon; this is the single most valuable general move-ordering improvement missing.
2. **`eval.rs` — add rook centralization bonus beyond open files** (Risk: low). Currently rook eval only gives `ROOK_OPEN` / `ROOK_SEMI` bonuses. Add explicit bonuses for rooks on central files (d, e, c) or rooks attacking multiple key squares. Expected +10–20 Elo.
3. **`eval.rs` — add knight outpost bonus** (Risk: low). Knights on squares attacked by no enemy pawns, defended by friendly pawns. Expected +15–30 Elo.
