### Shogun Sensei — chess analysis

**Game story:** Two near-perfect engines (ACPL 3 vs 5) traded down a balanced middlegame into a symmetric ending that was dead at 0.00 from move 32 to the finish, ending in threefold repetition. Chess-wise we made essentially no mistakes; the "loss" here is a ladder-design problem, not a blunder.

**Key moments:**
- Ply 40 (20...Na5, rim, self-eval +29) instead of SF's 20...Nb4 (centralizing, hits Bd3). White replied 21.b4 gaining space with tempo; objective swung +12 → −34 — a small blind spot about a rim knight's vulnerability.
- Plies 55–56 (28.Rxe6 Rxe6) and 65–66 (33.Re1 Rxe1): we accepted every rook trade with the eval already ~0, collapsing into a drawn ending. Theme: **no aversion to simplifying dead-equal positions**.
- From move 32 the eval curve is exactly 0.00; contempt only fires at terminal draw/repetition nodes.

**Suggestions (ranked):**
1. Contempt-scaled "keep tension" term: when |eval| < ~30 cp, penalize non-forced trades of non-pawn material. Expected impact: medium.
2. Raise Contempt 25 → ~40–50 cp, since draws score the same as losses on the ladder. Expected impact: low–medium.
3. Small penalty for a middlegame knight retreat to the rim. Expected impact: low.

### Engine Smith — engine analysis

**Diagnosis:**
- No search or eval bug found this game (ACPL 3). Ply 40 Na5 vs Nb4 is below the significant-error bar.
- Draw handling is the ladder-relevant gap: `draw_score()` only fires at rule-based terminal nodes (repetition / 50-move / insufficient material, and stalemate). Nothing influences the earlier decisions that simplify a level position into a dead line.

**Code suggestions (ranked by expected Elo / risk):**
1. `search.rs::Searcher::new` — raise `contempt: 25` to ~50–60 (and the UCI default). Risk: low.
2. `search.rs::think` root tie-break — among root moves within `contempt` of best and of 0, prefer the one keeping more non-pawn material. Risk: medium.
3. Validate with a win/draw/loss tally vs fixed-strength Stockfish rather than selfplay, which scores draws as 0.5.

### Verification (one skeptic per suggestion, against the code and the game record)

All six suggestions were refuted. The key correction came from reading the game record: **the 0.00 was full-strength Stockfish's score, not ours.** `evalCp` is stored from White's side, and our engine thought Black (us) was better throughout. At ply 92 it chose the queen trade 46...Qe4 at +2.15, then rated the resulting **pawn ending +2.26 to +2.61 for us at depth 24–27 for ~15 moves**, while it was objectively a fortress (0.00), e.g. `8/5kp1/2p5/6P1/2pPpP2/8/8/2K5 w`. It only dropped to exactly the draw score (−25) from ply 126, when every alternative was losing. So we didn't drift into a draw for lack of contempt; we simplified on purpose into a dead ending that our **evaluation wrongly thought was winning**.

## Decision
- **Implement next (v0.1.13):** the pawn-ending false positive both final verifiers identified — **rule of the square** in `eval.rs`: in a pawn-only ending, a passed pawn whose promotion square the enemy king can reach in time (Chebyshev distance − side-to-move tempo ≤ moves to promote) keeps only **1/4** of its passed-pawn endgame bonus (including v0.1.12's connected-passer bonus, which a verifier warned could worsen exactly this case). Build + selfplay after game 0013.
- **Result:** kept — v0.1.13 scored 6.5/16 (41%) vs v0.1.12 in selfplay (eval-only change). Static eval of the game-12 fortress `8/5kp1/2p5/6P1/2pPpP2/8/8/2K5 w` fell from +1.38 to +0.78 for Black (objective 0.00).
- **Backlog:** (1) W/D/L tally in selfplay output (diagnostic); (2) contempt via the runner's UCI option if a draw we could have avoided ever shows up; (3) lone-queen king danger; (4) knight outposts.
